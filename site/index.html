§02

patience, then paper

Curing & Rolling

Fresh buds are worth little. A drying rack turns them into cured product over about 4 minutes, and the rack is a decision rather than a timer.

StageWhat it means
0soaking wet — refuses to be collected
1–2collectable, but a grade lost per stage short
3peak — full grade, double yield
4overdried, one grade lost

Peak gets a long grace period before it spoils. You should have to forget about it, not lose it for stepping away.

Blends

The mixing station takes two to four kinds of dried bud and makes something that is none of them. Six strains taken two to four at a time is 203 distinct recipes, and a handful of them have names worth finding. Whole stacks go in; it runs the lot in one click.

Grade follows your worst slot. Averaging would let one Fire bud launder three Swill ones.

§03

Blends

Two to four kinds of dried bud go into the mixing station and come out as something that is none of them. Effects are the union of the parts, each scaled by its share — a mix that is three-quarters Kush feels mostly like Kush.

§04

three custom effects

The High

Baked and Wired are effects of their own, with their own icons and their own rules — not a stack of borrowed ones wearing a new name.

Baked

Burns saturation, then hunger — the munchies. Heals you slowly while you are well fed, and chips your health if you smoke on an empty stomach. Duration and level come from the strain and the grade.

Wired

The coca line's effect. Speed and resistance to sit down for; the comedown is the part you plan around.

Tolerance

Builds as you smoke and damps everything that follows, up to level 4. One level wears off in about 6 minutes. Chain-smoking your own stock is a losing move.

Ways to take it

MethodWhat it does
Jointbud + paper. The portable one, and what customers buy.
Bongwater bucket once, then a cured bud a hit. Water stays; the bowl does not.
Gravity bongwater, bud, flint and steel, then pull. The heaviest hit in the mod.

Hold right-click to smoke one. It plays out properly — the joint goes to your mouth, and everybody nearby sees the smoke.

§05

longer chain, richer end

The Coca Line

A bush ripens in about 20 minutes on any ground with light. The value is not in the farming — it is in what happens next.

  1. 01Bush2–4 leaves a harvest
  2. 02Leaf press5 leaves → paste
  3. 03Refinerpaste + blaze powder

Purity is decided entirely by when you pull it. Too early and it is cut; too late and it burns.

§06

a price list that breathes

The Market

13 shelves and over a thousand lines, priced by three forces multiplied together — and every one of them moves for a reason you caused.

Supply

The money in circulation, sampled from what players are carrying and what is sat in their chests. Every emerald spent leaves it; every payout enters it. A jackpot really is inflationary.

Drift

Each line walks its own path between random targets, eased so it arrives gently. Come back in a minute and copper has moved — in the direction it was already going. Up to ±18%.

Order flow

Buying pushes a price up, selling pushes it down, and it fades over the following minutes. Clear the shelf and the last one costs more than the first.

Prices never drop below 0.65× or climb past 1.85× of normal — and "normal" is whatever the last few hours looked like, not whatever the first day looked like. A good week becomes the new normal instead of leaving everything dear forever.

The counter buys back at 45% — wide on purpose. The shop is a convenience, not an income.

§07

the reason to walk across town

Stalls

The stall you place is yours. Put a chest directly underneath it and everything inside goes on sale to everybody else at 85% of the market price.

RouteGrower getsBuilder paysLost
The counter45e100e55e to nobody
A stall81e85e4e pitch fee

Both sides beat the counter and neither takes anything from the other — the spread that used to evaporate is split between you. There is no price editor: prices follow the market, so a stall is never stale and the only decision left is what to stock.

The market screen tells you when a neighbour has a line cheaper, with their name and coordinates. /stalls lists the lot.

§08

the public purse

The City

Nothing is taxed until somebody crafts a city vault and puts it down, and no house can be registered either — there is nobody to register it with. Put one down and both start, server-wide, with an announcement.

There is one vault and one purse. Every duty anybody pays goes into it, and anybody may spend it — every withdrawal is announced to everyone on the server. That is a decision, not an oversight: three friends can agree what the money is for in ten seconds, and a voting interface would be a menu standing where a conversation should be.

Breaking the vault spends nothing. The money is in the city's books, not in the block — it just means nobody can reach it, or file a house, until one is stood up again.

What is taxed

DutyOnStarts atBand

RentWhat a tenant pays their landlord6%2–14%

Buying pays a duty on top of the shelf price — and the shelf price you see already includes it, at the counter and at a neighbour's stall alike. Being paid has income duty taken out of it. Every stake laid on a casino floor pays gaming duty, win or lose, which is the only version that cannot be dodged by a lucky night.

Nothing sold to customers or dealers is taxed at all. That is not an oversight either — the black market pays better per hour precisely because it pays nothing to anybody, and that is a problem worth having.

What the purse buys

A treasury with no sink is a scoreboard. Each of these is bought once, permanently, by anybody, from the vault — and announced, the same rule as a withdrawal and for the same reason.

Every one of them only makes sense for a city. The roads are the one thing in the game that rewards building near each other; the watch is the city answering the thing that makes farms dangerous; the exchange pays everybody, including whoever never leaves their farm; and the school, the clinic and the tramway each make the people living in your houses worth more — better paid, slower to give up on a place, and more of them out shopping at once.

Shops the town walks into

A market shelf over a chest or barrel sells what is in it — not to players, but to the city. Townspeople come out of the housing, walk to the building, take a lot off the shelf and pay 90% of the market price, which is about double what the counter gives for the same crate. The duty on the sale goes straight to the purse.

A shop till is the shop. Put one down and every market shelf within 24 blocks joins it — no wand, no attaching, a shelf simply belongs to the nearest till. One name, one price policy, one cash register for the whole building, and stock is any chest or barrel under the till or under any of its shelves, so a back room and a stocked counter both work.

Prices are yours: from 75% to 135% of what the town expects to pay. Cheap brings more of them through the door; dear takes more off each one. Opening the till pays you the takings.

Somebody behind the counter

Hire a shopkeeper at the till for 45e a day, taken out of the takings. They stand at the counter, the shop draws far more custom, and — the real reason — it keeps trading while you are anywhere on the server instead of only when you happen to be stood nearby.

Not while you are logged off. A shop that held its chunk loaded forever would be a chunk loader you buy for 45e a day, and a server full of them is somebody else's tick budget. The crew works the same way for the same reason.

If the till cannot make the wage, they walk out and you are told. A shopkeeper is paid out of the shop, not out of your pocket, so a shop that sells nothing cannot quietly bleed you.

Over the counter

Shelves sell joints, cured buds and powder as well as groceries — at 65% of the street price, but clean: paid in real emeralds, declared, taxed, and nobody carries heat for it. Half again as much on the street, dirty, and it has to go through a drum. The safest money is the slowest.

How much custom you get is the population — the sum of the housing grades. So the loop closes: houses make people, people shop, shopping pays the farmer and the city, and the purse pays for more of the town. Nobody has to be told to build houses; the shop tells them. /shops lists the counters and the head count.

Townspeople buy food far more often than anything else, which is both true and the reason this is built for whoever is doing the farming.

The revenue office

Everything legal pays duty and everything illegal pays nothing, which on its own just means drugs are better. The office is the other end of that: it reads what came in against what you declared, and over 250e a day it cannot account for it assesses you for 35% of the excess. Cannot pay? The debt stands and you carry heat until it is settled — /law pay.

Dirty money

The street does not pay in emeralds. Customers and dealers pay in dirty emeralds — an item, not a balance. No shop takes them, no wage comes out of them, and the market does not know they exist. They are not money yet.

They stack and pack like the real thing: nine to a block and back again, and a big payout arrives as blocks already. The awkward part of dirty money is meant to be that it has to be washed, not that a week off the street is sixteen stacks to carry. The drum takes blocks too, each counting as nine.

They become money in a laundry drum: right-click it holding them, 2 at a minimum and 128 to a load, then wait — 3 seconds an emerald, so about 6 minutes for a full drum. Take it out and you get clean emeralds with up to 20% gone down the drain: the cut is rolled, so you never know quite how much. That is the moment those emeralds enter the money supply at all.

Time is per emerald rather than per load, so a drum is a throughput and not a free multiplier — and adding more restarts the clock, so load it all and walk away. If one is not enough, build another.

Washing also clears the day's exposure — but only up to what your businesses could plausibly have taken. A shop that sold nothing explains nothing, however much its owner would like it to. A real business is the licence to launder and its size is the limit, which is why a market shelf and a casino floor are worth owning for a reason other than what they earn.

Acts

The council passes laws when the city needs them and repeals them when it does not. Reactive, never random — a rule that arrives for no reason is weather, and nobody plays around weather. /law hands you the constitution, written fresh the moment you ask.

ActPasses when

The budget

Rates move on their own every 2 days and the change is announced with its reason. Under 600e in the purse and everything goes up; over 9000e and everything comes down; otherwise each rate wanders a point either way inside its band. /city prints the current table and what each duty has raised.

§08b

a room the city can see

Housing

Craft a mailbox, stand it inside a room once and right-click it. It walks the walls and tells you what you have built — and once it passes, that room is an address.

Then the box belongs outside. Sneak + right-click empty-handed and it comes into your hand carrying the address, so you can nail it up by the door or out on the street. Mining it works too. The survey stays pinned to the spot it was first taken from; the box is only where the post goes.

A box that has lost its address is not a problem: put a blank one back inside the house and it takes the job again, or stand one outside and it serves your nearest house that has no post. /homes demolish takes the house you are standing in off the register.

Sealed means sealed

Doors count as walls, which is what makes a bedroom with the door shut still part of your house: every door on the edge gets probed on its own, and one that opens onto something small is another room, while one that opens onto the world is your front door. Stairs and ladders make upstairs work with no extra thought.

A sealed void with no way in is not a house, so walling off a cavern to inflate the floor area does nothing.

The five musts

Miss any of these and it is not a house at all, whatever else is in it: sealed · 9 squares of floor · a bed · a door onto the street · a light.

Size is a lid, not a bonus

This is the part worth knowing. Floor area does not earn points — it decides the highest grade the place is allowed, and nothing else can lift it. A cupboard with a bed, a table, a chest, a furnace and a torch is a grade one, however neatly it is fitted out.

FloorHighest grade allowed

Floor means squares you could stand on, so every storey counts and a cathedral ceiling counts once. Three storeys of a modest cottage gets there as surely as one big hall.

Then it is points

WorthFor
0–2built, not dug — 60% then 90% of the shell made of worked material
0–3fittings — a crafting table, storage, a furnace, a market stall and a window; two earns one, four earns two, all 5 earns three

0–2lighting — measured at head height, brighter than 8; a fifth of the floor dim earns one, a twentieth earns two

Every two points is a grade, up to 8 — and then the floor caps it. The mailbox always tells you the single next thing to do, so you never have to read the table.

Dirt, sand, gravel, plain stone, cobble, logs and leaves are what the world hands you and count as dug. Everything you crafted, smelted, cut or dyed counts as built — including anything a mod ships as decoration.

Two houses cannot share ground — flats side by side are fine, and so is one above another. A house reaches at most 48 blocks from its mailbox, and it re-measures itself every couple of minutes, so knocking a wall through or taking the bed out shows up on its own. /homes lists everybody's.

How many live there

A house can hold more than one person. Three things decide how many, and whichever is smallest wins:

one bed each · 30 squares of floor each · a good enough grade.

So a fourth bed in a small grade-two room houses nobody extra — you need the space and the grade as well. One person always fits, however rough the place is.

This matters because everything else counts people, not houses. Every shopper at your till, every gambler on your casino floor and every emerald of income tax is per person. A four-bed house is four people coming through your door.

Payday

Your residents go out to work and get paid once a day, based on the grade of the house they live in. This is the only way new money comes into the town.

The city taxes their wages first — 10% to start — and that goes to the vault. Whatever is left goes into the town purse.

Rent is then paid out of that purse, into your mailbox, the same day. So the wage is anchored to the rent table — 3× the rent at the bottom of every grade — which guarantees a resident always clears their own landlord and leaves something over to spend.

Size counts too

The grade decides the money. On top of that, a bigger house of the same grade is worth more than a smaller one.

Without that, size would only count in jumps: 90 squares of floor makes a grade four, and so does 149, so the last sixty blocks you laid would have earned nobody anything.

A house at the biggest end of its grade earns halfway towards the next grade — never the whole way, so moving up a grade is always better than just building wider. Adding floor can never make anyone earn less.

Rent goes up the same way. Rent and wages are the same figure seen twice: what a resident gets paid, and what they hand you for the room. So a big house both earns its resident more and pays you more rent. Per resident, per day:

The top grade has no band above it to reach towards, so it gets one more of its own width — a palace out-earns a mansion, and past 740 squares you have hit the ceiling of the whole ladder.

And a bigger house holds more people, so size pays twice: the household total is this figure times the number of heads.

You collect rent by opening the mailbox — there is no second thing to click. A tenant who somehow cannot make rent pays none of it rather than part; the mood slide below is what eventually moves them out.

The town purse — why this matters to you

That leftover money is what your shops and casinos get paid with. The town can only spend what it has earned. If the purse is empty, people stay home — fewer customers at your shelves and fewer gamblers at your machines.

What counts is how much there is per person. Around 200e each means a comfortable town that shops normally, and more than that means it shops harder, up to a limit. Twenty people sharing a purse are well off; two hundred sharing the same purse are not.

So the whole thing is a loop:

better houses → better-paid residents → more customers in your shop → more tax in the vault → public works → a better city. Build well and you get paid three times: the rent, the tax that pays for the city, and the money those people spend with you. /city shows the purse.

Mood, and the letters

Tenants hold a mood out of 100. Dark corners and a falling grade wear it down, and an unhappy tenant pays less before they pay nothing — so a slide shows up in the money before it shows up as an empty house. Under 25 they are packing.

They write, and the letters are in the mailbox: "The light on the landing has gone." "There's something growing next door. I can smell it." That is the whole tutorial for this system, and it needed no page.

They are your customers too

Right-click a resident empty-handed and they tell you what they fancy — a strain's joints, cured buds, or powder — and what they will pay for it. Hold it and click them again and they buy, in dirty emeralds, exactly like a customer at the door. The fancy and the price are rolled fresh most days, and somebody who likes where they live pays a little over.

They gamble, too. A resident living near a wired machine walks in and plays it rather than a stranger appearing from nowhere — the same person who pays your rent, going home afterwards. Which is an argument for building the casino where people actually live. Their stake comes out of the same purse their wages went into, and what they win goes back to it.

And they go to work. About one townsperson in three that you see is walking to a job rather than to a counter — a shop till, a stall, a casino floor, the vault. A town's jobs are whatever has actually been built, so a village of houses and nothing else has nobody commuting. It is scenery: the wage was already paid from the housing register, whether or not anyone was stood there watching.

Not next door

A grow within scanning distance of somebody's front room empties it. A small one leaves them miserable and paying two fifths; anything bigger and they go. The plantation and the apartment block cannot be the same place — that tension is what the whole city design was built around, and it is the only thing in the mod that makes the two halves of it argue over the same ground.

§09

somebody to do the picking

The Crew

120e to take somebody on, then 40e every five minutes they are working, harvest or no harvest. 5 hands is all one operation will carry.

They work daylight only by default — at dusk they find a bed inside the patch and turn in — and the clock stops with them, so nights cost nothing. Put one on nights from the board and they never stop: +25% on the wage and a clock that runs all night, so about twice the packets an hour for about twice the work. They stop for a breather every 12 jobs, and the breather is a share of the shift rather than a flat minute, so a quick hand rests as briefly as it works. They will not tread your farmland back into dirt, pull a rack early, or bone-meal your own crops.

The patch stays awake wherever you are, as long as you are logged in — you do not have to stand over anybody. If one wanders off or something eats it, the whip on the crew board drags it back, and puts a trained replacement down if the body is gone.

Every hand has its own spot — wherever you were standing when you took them on — and it moves. Walk to the new field, open the board, and Work here instead moves the spot and the person with it, across worlds if you like. They forget the old bed and the old chest and find new ones.

One chest

This is the thing people get wrong. A hand uses the nearest container to their spot — that one and no other — for everything: what it harvests into, and what it draws from. Rolling wants cured buds and paper in that chest; fresh buds off the plant will not do and no crafting table is involved. A different container nearer their spot quietly becomes the one they use.

The crew board names the chest it is actually using and marks any taught job the chest cannot currently back, so you never have to guess which of the two it is.

Crews on file

/crew save <name> writes down who works where and everything they know; /crew load <name> buys the lot back onto the same patches for what it cost the first time. /crew plans lists them. If a crew ever walks over wages it files itself under walkout on the way out, so nothing is ever really lost — only paid for twice.

Everything you teach them puts the wage up. A hand you cannot keep busy is a hand losing you money. Miss a payday and you get a notice rather than a walkout: 4 paydays on nothing, about two days, and paying one packet writes the arrears off.

§10

being seen costs something

Heat & Raids

A grow in the open gets noticed. Heat is measured over a 11-block radius: ripe plants count 3, hidden 2, growing 1, occupied racks 1, presses and refiners 2.

TierHeatWho turns up
Tier 128+2 pillager, 1 vindicator
Tier 260+4 pillager, 2 vindicator
Tier 3108+5 pillager, 3 vindicator, 1 ravager
Tier 4175+6 pillager, 4 vindicator, 2 ravager

Past the top tier the squad stops growing but the clock keeps shortening — twice the cap is twice as often, down to a floor. Building the biggest field that fits is no longer free.

They search

A raid does not just swing axes. Raiders walk to your containers, open them, and take product — so hiding a stash underground is worth doing, splitting it across two buildings is worth doing, and standing between them and the chest is worth doing.

Sealing the grow in buys time, not safety. If they cannot find a way round they come through the wall. Obsidian stops them; dirt does not.

§11

paranoia, phones and people

The Street

Everything that is not a product line.

Paranoia

A meter that builds from heat, how high you are, darkness, night and being alone — and decays in daylight, sober, or near another player. Four tiers, from noises behind you to a motionless figure at render distance. None of it is real. Nothing is ever spawned, nothing touches your build, and nobody else can see it.

Contracts

A burner phone is a job board: the same five deliveries all day, so you can look at the board in the morning and plan around it. Jobs are advertised cold and the heat premium is settled at the drop — you are paid for risk you actually ran. Capped at 256e.

Reputation

It lives on the phone itself, so lending somebody your phone lends them your name — and losing it loses the standing with it. Capped at 50, because it feeds four multipliers at once and three of them used to be unbounded.

Dealers

The first thing you own that works while you are not there. They take a cut, sell far better at night than at noon, get in each other's way if you crowd a patch, and the cheap ones get robbed.

The Ledger

Book, compass and two amethyst. Reads every container within 32 blocks — shulker boxes included — and draws a line of light to whichever chest holds what you clicked.

Stickups

The farm raid comes for the plants. This comes for you, at the moment you hand product over in person. It is the price of dealing yourself instead of paying somebody else to.

§12

seven games and a vault

The House

A casino is a bankroll and a set of machines wired to it. The bankroll lives in the ledger, not on the card — the card is the key, so a machine can pay a winner at four in the morning while the owner is offline.

CabinetReturn to player
2×295.1%
3×396.2%
4×495.4%
5×597.1%

Roulette is a single-zero wheel: 37 pockets, and every bet on the table carries the same edge, so the choice is purely how you want to lose it. The Climb pays 96.5% at every rung — there is no correct place to stop, which makes it nerve rather than arithmetic.

Where the punters come from

The town. Punters are people and people live in houses, so trade scales with the housed population up to 12 grades — and a floor with no city behind it gets only the 15% who were walking past. Reputation and addiction still do the rest, and they still have to be earned.

Machines wear out, and it shows

Every cabinet gains a wear point about one round in 15, and dies at 100. Past 45 it starts swallowing money and sending punters back out of the door, so the hammer pays for itself in trade rather than in tidiness. The card shows the worst cabinet on the floor and you get told the moment one crosses the line.

A floor is a business, not a tap. Punters have to be served from your own stash, a pit boss costs a flat wage against a proportional skim, and an unattended floor earns very close to nothing.
